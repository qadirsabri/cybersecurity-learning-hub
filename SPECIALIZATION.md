# Specialization guide — Choosing a cybersecurity career path

[← Home](README.md) · [Monthly roadmap](ROADMAP.md)

## Recommended default: Cybersecurity Analytics

**Primary direction:** Security Data Analysis + Threat Detection. This combines Business Data Analytics skills (Python, SQL, statistics, data visualization) with cybersecurity evidence (logs, SIEM, alerts, investigations).

**Supporting skills:** SOC / Blue Team. **Optional direction:** Fraud / Cyber Risk Analytics. **Alternative:** GRC / IT Audit. Penetration testing requires additional dedicated technical preparation.

**The choice is provisional until November 2028.** The learner explores and reflects in October 2027, then makes a more informed selection in November 2028. Changing later is possible.

## Explore the career tracks

### 1. Security Data Analyst — default primary path
**What the work involves:** clean and analyze security events, investigate patterns, build dashboards, evaluate alerts and communicate findings.
**Useful skills:** Python, pandas, SQL, log formats, visualization, statistics, SIEM searches and detection evaluation.
**Beginner mini-task:** summarize a synthetic authentication-log CSV by user, time and event type; explain unusual patterns without claiming proof of an attack.
**Portfolio:** Projects 1 and 2; Project 4 can focus on detection quality.
**Good fit:** enjoys data, coding, investigation and explaining results.

### 2. SOC Analyst / Blue Team — supporting technical focus
**What the work involves:** monitor alerts, triage events, investigate evidence, document incidents and escalate when appropriate.
**Useful skills:** networking, Linux, Windows event logs, SIEM, packet analysis, incident timelines, ATT&CK mapping and clear reports.
**Beginner mini-task:** examine three synthetic alerts, rank by severity and write a short evidence-based triage note.
**Portfolio:** Project 3 Mini SOC Investigation Lab.
**Good fit:** enjoys investigating events, structured troubleshooting and operational teamwork.

### 3. Fraud / Cyber Risk Analytics — optional alternative
**What the work involves:** analyze suspicious transactions or behavior, evaluate risk signals, investigate anomalies and communicate trade-offs.
**Useful skills:** Python, SQL, data quality, statistics, risk scoring, anomaly detection, false-positive evaluation and responsible data handling.
**Beginner mini-task:** analyze a synthetic transaction table, flag simple unusual patterns and explain why flags are not proof of fraud.
**Portfolio:** Adapt Project 4 to fraud or risk analytics.
**Good fit:** enjoys business questions, statistical reasoning and decision support.

### 4. Cybersecurity GRC / IT Audit — optional alternative
**What the work involves:** assess security controls, maintain risk registers, collect audit evidence, map requirements and report gaps.
**Useful skills:** control frameworks, policy interpretation, risk assessment, audit sampling, evidence quality, spreadsheets, dashboards and communication.
**Beginner mini-task:** create a fictional company's asset list and five-row risk register, including control owners and evidence needed.
**Portfolio:** Adapt Project 4 to a controls assessment and audit dashboard; add dedicated GRC learning if selected.
**Good fit:** enjoys governance, documentation, business processes and structured assessment.

### 5. Penetration Testing — exposure, not current specialization
**What the work involves:** authorized security testing, careful validation of weaknesses and actionable reporting.
**Useful skills:** deeper networking, operating systems, scripting, web security, secure testing methods and report writing.
**Beginner mini-task:** complete an explicitly authorized PortSwigger training lab and write a defensive explanation.
**Portfolio:** The current roadmap offers introductory labs only; choosing this career requires a substantially different technical pathway.
**Good fit:** enjoys technical experimentation and extended hands-on lab work.

## Decision checkpoints

### October 2027 — Exploration reflection (not final)
1. Which activities felt most engaging: coding, dashboards, incident triage, risk analysis or audit writing?
2. Which of Projects 1 and 2 was more enjoyable? Why?
3. Which skills need more practice?
4. Which track should be explored in the next year?

Record answers in the [October 2027 monthly Issue](../../issues).

### November 2028 — Choose a primary track
1. Review Projects 1–3 and October 2027 reflection.
2. Compare at least three real entry-level job descriptions for each shortlisted track.
3. Select **one provisional primary track**, plus an optional supporting skill.
4. Record reasons, strengths, gaps, and the adjusted Project 4 topic in the [November 2028 Issue](../../issues).
5. Review the choice with the mentor.

**Certifications:** SC-900 and Security+ are options, not mandatory milestones. Choose according to the selected path, budget, prerequisites and current official exam objectives.

## Mentor review

Do not force a narrow choice during the first year. Ask for evidence of enjoyment, strengths and practical work. Support a reasonable pivot if interests change.

**Ethics:** all labs must be authorized. Use synthetic or licensed public data; never expose real credentials, private records or sensitive logs.
